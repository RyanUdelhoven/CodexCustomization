# Problem Discovery

Separate the proposed solution from the desired outcome without dismissing the user's idea. Explore who experiences the problem, a recent concrete occurrence, current workaround, frequency/impact, and what improvement would matter. Ask what evidence or observable result would establish success.

Useful probes: “Walk me through the last time this happened.” “What consumes the most effort today?” “What would become possible if this worked?” Distinguish user needs from agent assumptions and avoid requiring quantified metrics when a concrete qualitative outcome is enough.

Close with a problem statement, affected people, current approach, desired outcome, and constraints. Preserve explicit solution choices. Route unsettled feasibility or approach research to task-research rather than claiming an interview proves a solution works.
