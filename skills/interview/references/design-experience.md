# Design and Experience

Clarify the user's primary task, audience, information priorities, context of use, confusing interactions, and intended visual character. Inspect existing patterns and supplied references before asking about styles. Ask what specifically works in a reference rather than copying all its details.

Useful probes: “What should someone notice or do first?” “Which part of this screen currently slows you down?” “Should this emphasize quick scanning or detailed comparison?” Explore loading/error feedback, keyboard use, accessibility needs, and narrow layouts where they affect the experience.

Close with experience goals, information hierarchy, interaction decisions, relevant states, and observable usability criteria. Preserve project accessibility requirements and avoid horizontal scrolling. Saved styles and component reuse remain owned by their specialized skills; do not ask the user to choose imaginary library candidates.
