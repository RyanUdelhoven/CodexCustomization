# Review costs and alternative approaches

Treat the user's goal as the target and their proposed method as a starting point. Consider materially different ways to accomplish the same goal, including existing project capabilities, built-in tools, local implementations, and third-party solutions. Offer useful alternatives with practical tradeoffs. Honor an explicit requirement to use a particular method; do not silently substitute another approach or turn a routine task into an unnecessary survey.

## Compare options and verify costs

Prioritize free options that meet the requirements. Research the available options across relevant approach categories, including free alternatives to paid products. Complete the comparison before pausing for the cost discussion; do not stop at the first paid result. Compare materially different viable approaches. Stop when verified capabilities, costs, and tradeoffs support the choice; expand only for a consequential unresolved gap. Disclose limits without claiming market completeness.

Verify current pricing and licensing against official sources. Include a pricing source and access date for each candidate. Explain whether the proposed use is free, conditionally free, trial-only, paid, or unverified. Check subscriptions, usage charges, minimum commitments, required paid dependencies, hosting, commercial licenses, free-tier limits, overages, and automatic billing where relevant. Distinguish optional paid tiers from charges required by the proposed use. Do not call a free trial permanently free or equate open source with zero operating cost. State assumptions for estimates; never invent prices or promise zero cost when pricing remains unclear.

## Pause after cost research

If any researched option has any monetary cost, finish the research, then pause the workflow and tell the user before proceeding into selection, installation, integration, purchase, or billable execution. This pause applies even when free alternatives exist or the paid tier is optional; explain that distinction clearly. Present the completed options comparison, identify the cost and billing conditions, prioritize viable free choices, and ask how the user wants to proceed. Wait for their response. Research must itself use available, nonbillable access; do not incur charges to finish the comparison.

If pricing cannot be verified, report that uncertainty and resolve it with the user before adopting the option. If all researched options are verified free for the intended use, continue within the authorized task. After the user decides, honor that decision without repeatedly requesting the same approval; raise new costs or changed terms before proceeding.

## Coordinate with other workflows

This skill owns the user's free-first preference, alternative-method check, pricing disclosure, and cost pause. When research-backed-skill-builder applies, reuse its research and source checks instead of running a duplicate survey. Specialized skills own their implementation or artifacts. Plugin-management owns plugin discovery and connection mechanics; its instruction to continue independent work does not override this user's cost pause. Use source-specific skills when applicable. This workflow does not authorize purchases, new accounts, subscriptions, or external mutations.

When task-research applies, it owns task framing and the broader preference ranking; this skill owns pricing verification and the cost pause. Share one comparison and research pass. Finish and deliver the research report before asking about any subsequent paid choice; reporting alternatives does not itself authorize adoption.
