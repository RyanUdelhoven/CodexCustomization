# Present the analysis

Write a standalone explanation using direct statements and active voice. Avoid repeated presenter attribution. Preserve distinctions between verified facts, disputed claims, and interpretations through precise wording and linked evidence.

Begin with a short, direct paragraph stating the main takeaway and its implications. Do not label it “BLUF” or add a heading before it. Keep this opening within 30 words as well. Correct or qualify the video's thesis here when the evidence requires it.

Organize the main body under descriptive Markdown headers reflecting the refined topics. Consolidate related findings and omit standalone sections that add no distinct value to the user's question; do not reproduce the initial chapter outline or create one report section per internal analysis section. Add a section summary of no more than 30 words only when it helps orient the reader without repeating the opening or nearby content; otherwise begin directly with the substantive explanation. Optional bullets must add useful detail, with no more than 15 words per bullet. Preserve qualifications and corrections in supporting prose or separate supporting points instead of creating extra sections solely to meet summary limits. Do not include timestamps, time ranges, segment IDs, or timestamp-bearing video URLs in user-facing output. Preserve those internally for accuracy.

Link short evidence or uncertainty tags beside claims that need support: `[\[fact\]](SOURCE_URL)`, `[\[disputed\]](SOURCE_URL)`, `[\[uncertain\]](SOURCE_URL)`, or `[\[inference\]](PREMISE_SOURCE_URL)`. Choose tags matching the source's actual support. Use `\[unverified\]` without a link when no supporting source exists. A premise source does not independently verify an inference. Tags and URLs do not count toward word limits. Avoid redundant source lists.

Include a clearly headed conclusion only when it adds a useful assessment, decision, or practical next step beyond the body. Keep it within 50 words, excluding its header and epistemic tags. Ground any next step, test, or decision in the discussion's context. If the information is weak, inapplicable, or not useful, explain what to verify or avoid where relevant instead of inventing benefits. Do not assume personal circumstances absent from the conversation. Apply the same fact-checking and epistemic-tag rules.

Edit for information density: keep each substantive point once, combine overlapping examples, and remove sentences that repeat nearby content. Preserve evidence, qualifications, and disagreements; match the user's requested depth.

Before `finish`, check the opening, consolidated topic headers, applicable summary and bullet word limits, any conclusion's added value and 50-word limit, absence of visible timestamps and ads, and support for factual claims. A mechanical coverage check does not verify facts or enforce this prose format; Codex must perform these final checks.
